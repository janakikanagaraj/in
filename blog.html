June 02, 2026

A New Chapter: Perundevi is heading to the Big Screen!

My dear readers, the darkness is expanding. I am beyond thrilled to finally announce that the film rights to Perundevi — The Witch have been officially acquired by Castling Entertainment! The visionary director Anne Edwin will be directing the adaptation. This is a surreal moment for me. When I first wrote about the haunted village and the terrifying ley lines, I could only see them in my mind. Soon, we will all see them on the big screen. We finalized the deal for ₹12 Crore—a staggering number that proves horror fiction in India is finally getting the respect it deserves. Watch out for the trailer soon. The devils are waking up.

May 20, 2026

Reacting to India Today: The ₹12 Crore Deal

I read the piece in India Today calling the Perundevi deal 'the deal that will change how publishers value vernacular horror fiction.' It’s humbling. But for me, it was never about the money. It was about making sure Perundevi’s story was told exactly how I envisioned it—dark, uncompromising, and deeply rooted in our Tamil folklore. Anne Edwin promised me exactly that.

April 20, 2026

A Note on The Hindu Profile

The Hindu recently published a profile on me: 'The Reclusive Author Who Made Tamil Nadu Afraid of the Dark.' I laughed at 'reclusive.' I am not hiding from the world; I am simply listening to it. The quietest places hold the loudest secrets. Thank you to Sudha Raghunathan for the generous words.

March 16, 2026

Half a Million Copies: Thank You Ananda Vikatan

Ananda Vikatan just reported that Mouna Ezhuthukkal crossed 500,000 copies in its first week. I saw the pictures of the queues outside bookstores in Chennai and Madurai. I am speechless. Writing this book took a piece of my soul, and to see you all embrace the silence so eagerly... it means everything.

February 04, 2026

Scroll.in and the Evolution of Tamil Horror

A recent piece in Scroll.in discussed how my books have shifted the landscape of Tamil horror from cheesy ghost stories to psychological, mythos-driven terror. This is precisely my goal. Our mythology is terrifying. Our village deities are fierce. We don't need to borrow western tropes when we have so much darkness in our own history.

December 06, 2025

Dinamalar's Review of Saabavimoosanam

Dinamalar called Saabavimoosanam 'a masterclass in building dread.' I wrote that book during a particularly harsh monsoon, and I think the relentless rain outside my window bled into the manuscript. Thank you to the reviewers who saw the underlying tragedy beneath the horror.

October 27, 2025

BBC Tamil Interview Reflections

My sit-down with BBC Tamil was one of the most intense interviews I've done. They asked me where my ideas come from. I’ll tell you what I told them: I don't invent my monsters. I just translate their whispers.

August 08, 2025

The Silence that Birthed 'Mouna Ezhuthukkal'

Mouna Ezhuthukkal is finally out. This book was born from a period where I lost my voice for three days due to a throat infection. The absolute silence in my house became deafening. I started wondering: what if the silence isn't empty? What if it's full of things we just can't hear?

March 11, 2025

Writing 'Perundevi': A Journey into Witchcraft

Of all my books, Perundevi — The Witch terrified me the most while writing it. The research took me to remote villages where black magic is still whispered about behind closed doors. Some of the rituals described in the book are real. I suggest you don't read them aloud.

Exploring Teen Horror with 'Iravin Kuzhanthai'

I wanted to write something for younger readers. Iravin Kuzhanthai is my love letter to the YA horror genre. We all remember the terror of our school at night. I just added a demonic entity with red eyes to the mix.

March 26, 2024

The True Ancestral Home Behind 'Irulum Oliyum'

Many ask if the mansion in Irulum Oliyum is real. Yes, it is based on my great-grandfather's dilapidated Chettinad home. When I visited it as a child, I always felt the walls were watching me. Now, they are watching my readers.

Descending into 'Naragam'

Naragam was a departure for me. I wanted to move away from haunted houses and explore the concept of a literal portal to hell. The fiery imagery was exhausting to write, but creating a Tamil inferno was an incredible creative challenge.

February 20, 2023

The Curse of 'Saabavimoosanam'

Winning an award for Saabavimoosanam still feels surreal. This book is fundamentally about grief and the desperate lengths we go to for power. The skeletal hands rising from the earth are a metaphor... mostly.

Where It All Began: 'Aavi Ulagam'

Today marks the anniversary of my debut novel, Aavi Ulagam. Looking back at it, it’s raw and unpolished, but it has a primal energy. The midnight banyan tree in the story still stands in my hometown.

The Video Library That Shaped Me

Growing up, my father's video library was my sanctuary. While other kids watched cartoons, I was absorbing the gritty, terrifying atmosphere of 80s horror tapes. That magnetic distortion on VHS tapes still influences the texture of my writing.

May 26, 2022

Ley Lines and the Geometry of Fear

In my research for Perundevi, I discovered the concept of Ley Lines—invisible paths of energy that connect ancient sites. I believe fear travels along these same lines. It's geographical.

May 06, 2022

Why I Write in the Dark

People ask about my process. I write strictly between midnight and 4 AM. No music. No lights except my monitor. You have to invite the darkness in if you want to understand it.

April 16, 2022

The Village Deities of Tamil Nadu

Our village deities—the Ayyanars, the Karuppaswamys—are protective but fierce. In my stories, I often explore what happens when that fierce protection turns into something more... malevolent.

March 27, 2022

Writers Block or Demonic Interference?

Some days the words won't come. I like to joke that the spirits are censoring me. But truthfully, writing horror is draining. You have to immerse yourself in trauma and fear, and sometimes you just need to step back into the light.

March 07, 2022

The Psychology of the Jump Scare

In literature, you don't have sudden loud noises. The 'jump scare' has to be earned through atmosphere. It's about leading the reader into a false sense of security, then pulling the floor out from under them.

February 15, 2022

My Favorite Tamil Folklore

The legend of the 'Katteri' (vampiric spirit) has always fascinated me. It's so much more terrifying than western vampires because it’s deeply tied to our specific cultural fears regarding childbirth and the wilderness.

January 26, 2022

Creating the Perfect Horror Protagonist

A good horror protagonist must be flawed. Fear only works when the character has something to lose, and secrets they are hiding. Perfect people are boring to torture.

The Aesthetics of Fear

Blood is cheap. Rust, rot, dampness, the smell of old copper, the sound of a fingernail tapping glass—that is true horror. It's sensory.

On the Idea of Haunted Objects

We attach so much memory to objects. Is it so hard to believe that intense emotions—grief, rage—could leave a residue? I collect old brass items, and sometimes, they feel... heavy.

Why Do We Love to be Scared?

It's the adrenaline of survival without the actual danger. Reading a horror novel is like a vaccine for real-world anxieties.

The Sound of the Night

If you listen closely to the Tamil Nadu countryside at 2 AM, it is never silent. There is a continuous, low hum of insects, wind, and things we cannot see. I try to capture that hum in my prose.

Finding Horror in the Mundane

The scariest things aren't monsters in the closet. The scariest things are familiar things acting slightly wrong. A door that was locked now open. A reflection that blinks a second too late.
